Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Expert versus Data-Learned Fuzzy Rules for Cognitive-Offloading Risk in Student–LLM Tutoring Dialogues: A Cross-Semester Evaluation", as an original research article for Applied Soft Computing.

Students who use large language model assistants can submit correct work while handing the reasoning to the model, and course grades cannot tell this cognitive offloading apart from genuine learning. The behavioural evidence is graded and imprecise, which makes fuzzy rules a natural tool. The manuscript asks a question that matters for any applied fuzzy system in a new domain: should the rules come from experts or be learned from data, and does either kind hold for a new cohort?

We answer it on the public StudyChat corpus of real student–ChatGPT dialogues (464 weakly labelled units from 175 students over two semesters, CC BY 4.0). A five-rule expert Takagi–Sugeno–Kang rule base fails as a standalone score (AUC {{S.f_auc}}) and as a Łukasiewicz implication constraint on a Transformer (ΔAUC {{S.imp_d}} over ten paired seeds), because its main antecedent, unchecked acceptance of generated code, is inversely associated with the outcome (ρ = {{S.e6_v_rho}}). A count-based rule base with eight grid rules and consequents learned in closed form then passes a cross-semester confirmation whose hypotheses were committed to the code repository before it was run. Trained on Fall 2024 and scored once on Spring 2025, with disjoint students, it reaches AUC {{S.tp_cf_tsk}} (98.75% CI {{S.tp_h2.lo}} to {{S.tp_h2.hi}}). This is non-inferior to a logistic regression on all dialogue-act counts, and the reverse direction gives {{S.tr_cf_tsk}}. Its eight learned consequents form a readable rule table whose main pattern holds in both directions.

We also report what did not work: the fuzzy constraint adds at most a few thousandths of AUC to the Transformer, and a rank fusion with the regression does not replicate in the reverse direction. Section 9 states the remaining threats, chiefly that the rule inputs were chosen with pooled data and that both cohorts come from one course.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. No new human-subjects data were collected.
