Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Fuzzy Rule-Constrained Sequential Learning for Detecting Cognitive Offloading in Student–Large Language Model Tutoring Dialogues", as an original research article for Applied Soft Computing.

Students who use large language model assistants can submit correct work while handing the reasoning to the model. Cognitive science calls this cognitive offloading, and course grades cannot detect it, because genuine learning and delegation both produce a passing submission. The behavioural evidence an instructor would reason with, such as the share of generated code accepted without a following check, is graded and imprecise: a student who accepts 48% of outputs unchecked is not categorically different from one who accepts 52%. This is the kind of problem soft computing is built for, and the manuscript treats it that way.

The method combines a Transformer sequence encoder with a five-rule zero-order Takagi–Sugeno–Kang fuzzy rule base. Membership breakpoints are fitted on each training fold, so the rule base never sees test data. The rules enter training as Łukasiewicz implication constraints, which penalise the network only where a rule fires, and the same firing degrees form the explanation attached to each risk score. We compare this implication loss under paired ten-seed tests with an unconstrained encoder, a linear rule target and a TSK regression target, and we evaluate the rule base on its own: its out-of-fold ranking power against a crisp version of the same rules, and its sensitivity to membership breakpoints.

The study uses real student–ChatGPT dialogues from the public StudyChat corpus (464 weakly labelled units from 175 students, CC BY 4.0), with a weak label built from the gap between AI-assisted assignment and unaided exam performance. An interpretable dialogue-act logistic regression reaches AUC 0.654 (permutation p < 0.002) and the unconstrained Transformer {{S.none_m}} over ten seeds; the two models are complementary rather than redundant (Spearman ρ = 0.45).
