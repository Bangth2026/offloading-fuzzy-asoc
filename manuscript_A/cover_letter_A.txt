Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Check the Premises Before Imposing the Rules: An Audit of Fuzzy Rule Models on Three Student–AI Interaction Datasets", as an original research article for Applied Soft Computing.

Fuzzy rule bases are a natural choice for modelling how students work with large language model assistants: the evidence is graded, and instructors want reasons they can read. The manuscript shows that this readability can hide a failure. A rule model can fit the structure of a course and still produce rules that sound behavioural. We propose a three-check audit for fuzzy rule models of interaction data and build two of its parts into rule construction: antecedents enter the rule base only if their sign holds within structural cells and within students, and consequents can be constrained to respect that sign. Every model is then compared with structure, label-persistence and student-propensity baselines and tested across cohorts under analysis plans written before each run.

The three studies give results of different kinds, and we report them with equal weight. On StudyChat the audit overturned our own earlier result: a model that knows only the assessment group ranks students better (AUC {{S.grp_p.e}} and {{S.grp_r.e}}) than any behavioural model, including a learned TSK rule base that had passed a planned cross-semester test. On TutorTrace a sign-checked eight-rule TSK classifier for answer-seeking queries transfers to a new programming task, its inputs keep their sign within students, and on that task it beats label persistence by {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.sc_minus_markov.e}} AUC; it still trails regression on all features. On RECIPE4U the same classifier beats course structure by {{S.r1.H2.e}} AUC, but a one-line persistence rule comes within {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.sc_minus_markov.e}} of it, so we read that result as persistence of the student's own requests rather than as a new behavioural signal.

We wish to be transparent about the history of this work. A related manuscript built on StudyChat alone, with a different method and different conclusions, was submitted to Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence (EAAI-26-29396) and declined at the editorial stage; it is not under review anywhere. An earlier draft of the present study was archived with its code on Zenodo (release v1.0.0, 4 November 2026); its behavioural conclusions are withdrawn in this manuscript, which explains why. The analysis plans are described as time-stamped written plans, not as formal pre-registrations: Section 3.2 and Table 1 state, for each plan, whether its push to the public repository can be verified from the host's own records to precede the run.

The code, analysis plans and statistics files are openly available at {{S.repo_url}} and archived on Zenodo (https://doi.org/{{S.doi}}, release v1.2.0). The three datasets are third-party and are not redistributed; RECIPE4U was obtained under its research-use terms and its texts are used only through their lengths, alongside the request labels, ratings and edit flags. No new human-subjects data were collected. I declare no competing interests, and the work received no specific funding.

Reviewers with expertise in fuzzy rule-based modelling, interpretable machine learning or learning analytics would be well placed to evaluate the work.
