Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Check the Premises Before Imposing the Rules: An Audit of Fuzzy Rule Models on Three Student–AI Interaction Datasets", as an original research article for Applied Soft Computing.

Fuzzy rule bases are a natural choice for modelling how students work with large language model assistants: the evidence is graded, and instructors want reasons they can read. The manuscript shows that this readability can hide a failure. A rule model can fit the structure of a course, the propensity of a student or the persistence of a label and still produce rules that sound behavioural. We propose a three-check audit for fuzzy rule models of interaction data: antecedent signs within structural cells and within students, comparison with structure, persistence and propensity baselines, and transfer across cohorts under written analysis plans.

The fuzzy contribution is specific. Two checks of the audit are built into Takagi–Sugeno–Kang construction: a split-half sign check selects antecedents, and linear constraints on the consequents make the output provably monotone in the selected directions (Proposition 1), placing the method within the literature on monotone TSK identification. A simulation with a known generating process shows that a planned transfer test accepts structural, propensity and persistence shortcuts as behaviour, while the audit accepts only a true within-student effect. On three public datasets the audit gives three different verdicts: on StudyChat a model of assessment group alone outranks every behavioural model (AUC {{S.grp_p.e}} and {{S.grp_r.e}}); on TutorTrace the rules for answer-seeking queries keep their sign within students but session structure explains much of the same signal; on RECIPE4U the rule base reduces to a one-line persistence rule. We report these results with equal weight, because the contrast between them is the point of the audit.

We wish to be transparent about related work. An earlier manuscript by the author, submitted to Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence (EAAI-26-29396), used StudyChat, the expert rule base and a rule-consistency regulariser on a Transformer encoder. That manuscript has been withdrawn and is not under consideration anywhere. The present manuscript shows that the signal it reported is largely assessment-group structure; the overlapping components appear here only as objects of the audit and are summarised in the supplementary material. An earlier draft of the present study was archived with its code on Zenodo (release v1.0.0, 4 November 2026); its behavioural conclusions are withdrawn. All analyses are reported as exploratory: their plans were written and committed before each run, but none was registered with a third party, and Section 3.2 states for which plans the public push can be shown to precede the run.

The code, analysis plans and statistics files are openly available at {{S.repo_url}} and archived on Zenodo (release {{S.release}}: https://doi.org/{{S.vdoi}}). The three datasets are third-party and are not redistributed; RECIPE4U was obtained under its research-use terms and its texts are used only through their lengths, alongside the request labels, ratings and edit flags. No new human-subjects data were collected. I declare no competing interests, and the work received no specific funding.

Reviewers with expertise in fuzzy rule-based modelling, interpretable machine learning or learning analytics would be well placed to evaluate the work.
