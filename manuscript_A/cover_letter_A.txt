Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Check the Premises Before Imposing the Rules: A Pre-Registered Audit of Fuzzy Rule Models on Three Student–AI Interaction Datasets", as an original research article for Applied Soft Computing.

Fuzzy rule bases are increasingly used to model how students work with large language model assistants, because the evidence is graded and instructors want reasons they can read. The manuscript shows that this readability can hide a failure: a rule model can fit the structure of a course and still produce rules that sound behavioural. We propose a three-check audit for fuzzy rule models of interaction data (antecedent signs within structural cells, a structure-only baseline, and cross-cohort transfer under an analysis plan committed before the run) and apply it to three public datasets.

On StudyChat, the audit overturned our own earlier result: a model that knows only the assessment group ranks students better (AUC {{S.grp_p.e}} and {{S.grp_r.e}}) than any behavioural model, including a learned TSK rule base that had passed a pre-registered cross-semester test, and within groups no behavioural input is related to the offloading proxy. On TutorTrace and RECIPE4U, where the target is the student's request itself, a sign-checked eight-rule TSK classifier transfers across classroom deployments and writing courses. In programming it does not clearly exceed session structure and costs about 0.08 AUC against regression on all features; in EFL writing it exceeds structure by {{S.r1.H2.e}} AUC and is non-inferior to the all-feature model. We report the negative and positive results with equal weight, because the contrast between them is the point of the audit.

All analyses after the initial experiments were specified in writing and committed to a public repository before they were run; the manuscript lists each plan and result with its commit hash. The code, plans and statistics files are openly available at {{S.repo_url}} and archived on Zenodo (https://doi.org/{{S.doi}}). The datasets are not redistributed.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. No new human-subjects data were collected. I declare no competing interests, and the work received no specific funding.

Reviewers with expertise in fuzzy rule-based modelling, interpretable machine learning or learning analytics would be well placed to evaluate the work.
