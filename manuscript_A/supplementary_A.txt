# S1. Study 1 analyses before the audit (E1–E12)
All analyses in this section are exploratory. The cross-semester analyses use the training semester's 75th percentile of y as threshold; pooled cross-validation uses the pooled 75th percentile ({{S.s1_thr}}).

The author-specified rule base FR1–FR5 reads four unit-level shares: v, the share of writing requests not followed by an editing or verification turn; u, the share of turns in runs of three or more assignment-specific questions; p, the share of provide-context turns; and e, the share of verification turns. Its consequents are fixed by the author (FR1: v HIGH → risk 1.0; FR4: v LOW and e HIGH → risk 0.0; and so on). It ranks units at chance in pooled five-fold cross-validation (AUC {{S.f_auc}}; {{S.c_auc}} for a crisp version) and across semesters ({{S.ex_p}} and {{S.ex_r}}). As a Łukasiewicz constraint on a Transformer it leaves AUC unchanged (Δ = {{S.imp_d}}, Holm-adjusted p = {{S.imp_p}}). Its main antecedent appears to have the wrong sign in pooled data: v is inversely associated with y (ρ = {{S.v_rho}}, 95% CI {{S.v_lo}} to {{S.v_hi}}). Learning the consequents on the same shares does not help ({{S.oc_auc}}). With log counts of writing requests (W), conceptual questions (C) and provide-context turns (P) and learned consequents, CF-TSK trained on Fall 2024 reaches {{S.cf_p}} on Spring 2025 (98.75% CI {{S.cfci_p.lo}} to {{S.cfci_p.hi}}) against {{S.lr8_p}} for a regression on all eight broad counts, and {{S.cf_r}} in the reverse direction. Moving the input selection inside the training semester gives {{S.nest_p}} and {{S.nest_r}}. In pooled cross-validation an LSTM is the most accurate encoder ({{S.lstm_m}} ± {{S.lstm_sd}} over ten seeds, against {{S.lr_m}} ± {{S.lr_sd}} for the regression and {{S.tf_m}} ± {{S.tf_sd}} for a Transformer); across semesters it reaches {{S.lstmx_p}} and {{S.lstmx_r}}.

On counts, author-set directions rank units below chance ({{S.ec_p.e}}, CI {{S.ec_p.lo}} to {{S.ec_p.hi}}), and learning the consequents raises AUC by {{S.cfec_p.e}} (CI {{S.cfec_p.lo}} to {{S.cfec_p.hi}}). Adjusting for the preceding unaided exam, CF-TSK keeps a positive coefficient from Fall to Spring ({{S.prior_p.b}}, CI {{S.prior_p.lo}} to {{S.prior_p.hi}}) but not in the reverse direction ({{S.prior_r.b}}, CI {{S.prior_r.lo}} to {{S.prior_r.hi}}). Replacing up to 40% of dialogue-act labels at random leaves the frozen rule base's AUC between {{S.noise_lo}} and {{S.noise_hi}}. The effect of rule constraints on neural encoders is negligible: the interval of the AUC difference lies within ±0.01 for the LSTM in both directions and for the Transformer from Fall to Spring ({{S.con.primary.transformer.d}}, CI {{S.con.primary.transformer.lo}} to {{S.con.primary.transformer.hi}}).

For rule injection into neural encoders we read rule j as "if the antecedent holds, risk is about z_j" and penalise violations of the Łukasiewicz bounds w_j z_j ≤ r ≤ 1 − w_j(1 − z_j) with a hinge loss. A rule that fires fully pins r to z_j; when several rules fire fully with different consequents the bounds conflict, and the loss settles where the summed violation is smallest. Every encoder is also trained with binary cross-entropy, so the constraint is never satisfied exactly. Encoders: Transformer with two layers, two heads, width 32 and an assessment token; LSTM with one layer of width 32; 15 epochs, Adam (10⁻³), batch 32.

Seven planned tests used Spring 2025 as the held-out semester: E10 H1 (CF-TSK non-inferior to LR8), H2 (CF-TSK above chance), H3 (rule constraint improves a Transformer) and H4 (fusion of CF-TSK and LR8 improves on LR8); E11 H1 and H2; and E12 H5 (rule constraint improves an LSTM). Under Bonferroni correction at 0.05/7, E10 H1, H2, H4, E11 H1, H2 and E12 H5 hold and E10 H3 does not; E12 H5 holds with a mean gain of about 0.001 AUC. These decisions are reported for completeness; the plans were not public before the runs.

One step of the audit had to be corrected. A first attempt to remove group effects thresholded the label within each semester × group cell. In Fall 2024 group 2 the cell's 75th percentile is zero, so that rule marks every unit of the cell positive and reintroduces the group effect (results/revision_b_stats.json, key two_by_two). The within-group conclusions of the paper rest only on rank correlations with the continuous y.
