# Report A

**M1. Check 3 inconsistent.** Plan provenance is now separate from Check 3. Check 3 is the statistical transfer criterion, and provenance is reported beside the verdict (Algorithm 1, line 11; Section 3.2). Figure 1, Sections 5.2 and 9 and Table 6 follow this definition.

**M2. Check 2 not incremental.**
- Check 2 is now the gain of b_all + f over b_all, where b_all combines structure, persistence and propensity in one logistic regression (Table 1).
- Incremental gains over each baseline alone are secondary (Table S11).
- The simulation was rerun under this definition. Under persistence the audit now detects β = 1 in {{S.syn5['C_1.0_3-5_none'].behavioural}}% and {{S.syn5['C_1.0_6-10_none'].behavioural}}% of replications with 3–5 and 6–10 requests, and power rises with session length.
- The conclusions for Study 2 changed, as described above.

**M3. Positive control and competitor.**
- A student fixed-effects model is now the reference in every simulated condition (Figure 5). It has similar false-positive rates and more power in short sessions, and Section 9 says so.
- Eight departures from the base design are tested (Table 7): non-monotone and threshold effects, 15% label noise, measurement error, three sessions per student, a correlated non-causal candidate, 20% missing requests and a time-varying effect.
- The audit holds up except for the non-monotone effect, where both it and the fixed-effects model fail.
- We know of no real dataset with an established behavioural antecedent for these targets. In that sense the D1 → D2 verdict is the closest thing to a positive case, and we present it as fragile.

**M4. Scope.**
- The claims are narrowed. The title is now "… Auditing Zero-Order TSK Rule Models of Student–AI Interaction", and the abstract and Section 10 state the model class.
- FURIA was run through Weka (jars from Maven Central) on the three SC-TSK inputs and on all candidates. It learns 2–5 rules and is inconclusive in every split.
- Genetic fuzzy systems and first-order TSK were not run; Section 10 says so.

**M5. A construction aligned with the audit.** RF-TSK fits b_all first. It then fits the consequents to the residual, using within-student deviations as inputs (Section 8). It fails Check 2 on D2 and D1 and is inconclusive elsewhere. We report this as a negative result and explain it: much of SC-TSK's advantage over the baselines on D2 comes from first queries, which have no earlier request to deviate from.

**M6. Study 1 label.**
- With y residualised within semester × group, group-only falls to {{S.r5.s1.f24_to_s25.group_only.e}}/{{S.r5.s1.s25_to_f24.group_only.e}}.

# Report B

**1. Decision table.** Table 1 gives the outcomes of each check and the combination rule; a fail or contradiction overrides passes.
**2. Broader simulation.** Table 7 and Figure 5 cover several session lengths, effect sizes and confounds, plus hierarchical structure (cells with student intercepts), label noise, measurement error, missing records, time-varying and non-linear effects, several sessions and a correlated confounder. Varying the random-effect variance was not added.
**3. Three-way verdict.** This is adopted (Table 1). Under the incremental check the audit is no longer conservative under persistence at β = 1.
**4. Structural and mixed variables.** The definitions and the adjudication rule are in Section 3.1 and Table 2. Test progress decides the size of the gain over structure on D2; Section 5.2 reports both classifications.
**5. Label quality.** In the simulation, label noise of 15% leaves the false-positive rate at {{S.syn5['AB_0.0_6-10_labelnoise'].behavioural}}% and power at {{S.syn5['AB_0.5_6-10_labelnoise'].behavioural}}% (Table 7). Section 9 adds that a structurally defined label cannot be audited into a behavioural one.
**6. Transfer protocol.** Section 3.1 now states the purpose of Check 3, how cohorts are defined, that nothing is refitted on the target, how base rates are handled, and that transfer is not evidence against shortcuts.
**7. SM-TSK.** In the simulation, a wrong direction drives SM-TSK to chance and a non-monotone effect escapes it. Under label noise it matches SC-TSK (Table 7). Monotone gradient boosting gives similar AUC (Section 8).
**8. Support threshold.** Counts at 5, 10, 20 and 30 cases and bootstrap intervals of the consequents are reported. Rules with intervals wider than 0.5 are marked unstable.
**9. Prediction versus explanation.** A new paragraph in Section 9 states what a behavioural verdict does and does not establish.
**10. Reproducibility.** A machine-readable manifest (manuscript_A/manifest.json) links each table and figure to its plan, code and result files. Every number is rendered from those files by one script.

**Minor comments.** The title is now descriptive. The scope (binary labels) and the treatment of ties, zero-variance inputs, the quartiles, w0 and z0 are stated in Sections 3.1 and 3.3. Rank correlation is justified in Section 3.1. Calibration is reported for all splits (Table S8). No student identifier is shared between the TutorTrace deployments or between the RECIPE4U course groups. We did not add precision, recall and F1, because the audit concerns ranking and no alert threshold has been validated with instructors.
