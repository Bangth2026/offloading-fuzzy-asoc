# Major comments

**M1. Soft-computing novelty and monotone fuzzy modelling.** (i) A new Section 2.3 reviews monotone and constraint-based fuzzy models: parameter conditions for monotone TSK systems [31], monotone zero-order TSK identification, relabelling and interpolation by Tay, Lim and co-workers [32–34], monotone Mamdani–Assilian models [35] and shape-constrained additive models [36]. (ii) Section 2.3 now states that SM-TSK is an instance of constrained identification of a zero-order TSK system, and that what it adds is the source of the directions (a within-sample sign check) and its role in the audit. Section 3.3 adds Proposition 1 with a proof. For the complementary-ramp product grid, Σ_j w_j(x) = 1, so monotone consequent pairs give a monotone output, and the background term only shrinks the output toward z_0 by w_0/(1 + w_0) ≈ 0.048. Table 7 adds a test-data measure of output monotonicity. SC-TSK moves against a selected direction for {{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.mono.sc}}–{{S.r3.S2_D2_to_D1.mono.sc}}% of small perturbations; SM-TSK never does. (iii) We did not add new monotone comparators. Logistic regression on the same three inputs is already in Tables 4 and 5 and is monotone by construction, and it performs within a few hundredths of SC-TSK. Section 9 now answers the question directly. We recommend fuzzy rule models for this task only together with the audit, and we state on what grounds: readability is cheap here, the monotone variant carries a guarantee, and the specific risk is that the rule text disguises a shortcut.

**M2. Check 1 with few requests per student.** The estimator is now defined in Section 3.1. Ranks are scaled to [0, 1] within each student, so every student is centred at 0.5; students with one request are dropped, and single-class students contribute nothing to the covariance. Section 5.2 and Supplementary Table S4 report the number of contributing students. For example, on D1 {{S.r3.S2_D1_to_D2.fe.pre_net_code_growth.counts.students_ge2}} students have two or more queries and {{S.r3.S2_D1_to_D2.fe.pre_net_code_growth.counts.students_both_classes}} of them have both labels. Conditional (fixed-effects) logistic regression by student gives negative coefficients with intervals excluding zero for every selected input, on the training and test sets of all three Study 2 splits (Table S4). In Study 3 all three inputs are now reported. Only the lagged-label input keeps its sign within students; the reply length and the number of earlier turns do not. Section 4.3 now notes that the 23% ties at zero in Study 1 reduce power.

**M3. Dependence on one random split.** We repeated the sign check with 200 seeds (Supplementary S4). Study 2 selects the same three inputs in 200 of 200 splits in all three directions. Study 3 SW → AW + IRW is unstable. The lagged-label input is always eligible, but the published set recurs only {{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.stab.same}} times, and the most frequent set ({{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.stab.top[1]}} times) swaps the number of earlier turns for the essay length before the request. Held-out AUC has median {{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.stab.med}} ({{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.stab.lo}}–{{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.stab.hi}}). Section 6.2 says that a rule table shown to an instructor would change between runs in its second and third inputs. The stability-selection variant (eligible in at least 80% of splits) is reported in S4.

**M4. Structure/behaviour boundary.** Section 3.1 now gives an explicit criterion: a variable is structural if it is fixed by course design, record keeping or the clock independently of how the student works. Table 1 applies the criterion to every variable and lists the debatable ones. By this criterion test progress is behavioural. Without it, structure alone reaches {{S.r3.S2_D1_to_D2.C.st.e}} on D2, and the H2 gain becomes {{S.r3.S2_D1_to_D2.C.h2.e}} (98.33% CI {{S.r3.S2_D1_to_D2.C.h2.lo}} to {{S.r3.S2_D1_to_D2.C.h2.hi}}), which meets the decision rule; on D1 and D3 it does not. Section 5.2 reports both partitions, and Section 9 names the partition as a choice that decides the verdict on one deployment.

**M5. Selective framing.** Highlight 4 now reads "In programming, rules keep their sign within students but add little to structure." The abstract no longer quotes the D3 gain over persistence. Section 5.2 calls the D3 comparison the least informative one in the study. It explains that persistence has no parameters, so its AUC of {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.auc_markov.e}} means the unvalidated D3 labels show no positive dependence between consecutive queries. It also states that structure alone ranks D3 queries as well as SC-TSK.

**M6. Study 3 as a persistence model.** The conclusion is now drawn firmly in Section 6.2, the abstract, highlight 5 and the conclusion: "Study 3 provides no evidence that the behavioural inputs describe production requests." (i) Flipping earlier labels at random, as an intent classifier would, degrades SC-TSK and persistence together. With 10% errors they reach {{S.r3.noise['0.1'].SW_to_AW_IRW.sc}} and {{S.r3.noise['0.1'].SW_to_AW_IRW.markov}}; with 30%, {{S.r3.noise['0.3'].SW_to_AW_IRW.sc}} and {{S.r3.noise['0.3'].SW_to_AW_IRW.markov}} (Table S5). A real intent classifier was not trained because the texts are used only through their lengths under the dataset's terms. (ii) Among the {{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.cond.n}} test requests with no earlier production request, SC-TSK reaches {{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.cond.sc.e}} (CI {{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.cond.sc.lo}} to {{S.r3.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.cond.sc.hi}}), and the other two inputs alone are at or below chance.

**M7. Weak prior on consequents.** The reviewer is right, and Section 3.3 has been corrected. With α = 1 the prior pulls only rules that almost no case fires, and a rule with little support at degree ≥ 0.5 can still receive an extreme consequent from partial firing. The caption of Figure 3 has been corrected too. Figures 3b and 4b now hatch cells with fewer than ten supporting cases and label them "n < 10". With α = N/10 (Section 8, Table S6) the weakly supported consequents move toward the base rate; for example, the five-query D1 cell moves from {{S.r3.S2_D1_to_D2.aN.low[4].z1}} to {{S.r3.S2_D1_to_D2.aN.low[4].zN}}. Held-out AUC changes little, except in the Study 3 reverse split ({{S.r3.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.aN.auc.e}}). We keep α = 1 and recommend marking such rules as extrapolated rather than showing them as findings.

**M8. Analysis history.** All analyses are now labelled exploratory throughout, including the abstract and the decision rules of Sections 5 and 6, which are described as planned decision rules rather than confirmatory tests. Table 2 is shortened to analysis, data, whether the plan was public before the run, and status. Commit times and hashes have moved to Supplementary Table S2. The R2 and R3 rows now say "internal review", and Section 3.2 states that both rounds were internal reviews before submission. The narrative of earlier drafts has been removed from the Introduction; Study 1 is described as "a count-based TSK model that passes a planned cross-semester transfer test".

**M9. Relation to the author's other work.** EAAI-26-29396, which used StudyChat, the expert rule base and a rule-consistency regulariser on a Transformer, has been withdrawn. The cover letter states this and explains how the two manuscripts relate. Section 1 also says that these components appeared in an earlier, withdrawn manuscript and appear here only as objects of the audit.

**M10. Fit with the journal and length of Study 1.** Study 1 is compressed to the audit result: a five-row Table 3, Figure 2 and the within-cell analysis. E1–E12, the neural encoders with rule injection, the planned tests on Spring 2025 (the former Appendix B) and the failed within-cell thresholding have moved to Supplementary S1. The space goes to Section 2.3, Proposition 1 and a new Section 7, which validates the audit on simulated data with a known generating process. Naive transfer calls x1 behavioural in {{S.r3.syn.S0.naive_behavioural}}%, {{S.r3.syn.S1.naive_behavioural}}%, {{S.r3.syn.S2.naive_behavioural}}% and {{S.r3.syn.S3.naive_behavioural}}% of replications in the structure, propensity, true-effect and persistence scenarios. The audit calls it behavioural in {{S.r3.syn.S2.audit_behavioural}}% of the true-effect replications and in at most {{S.r3.syn.S1.audit_behavioural}}% of the others. Section 7 notes that the simulated effect is strong, so power for weaker effects will be lower.
