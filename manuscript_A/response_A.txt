# Major comments

**M1. Conclusions and the decision rule.** We adopted option (b), and the verdict rule now reports each check separately (Algorithm 1, line 13). A rule base is called behavioural only if all three checks pass. Sections 5.2 and 6.2 now end with an "Audit verdict" paragraph. For Study 2: Check 1 passes, Check 2 fails (on D2 it passes against structure, gain {{S.r4.S2_D1_to_D2.sc_gaps.structure.e}}, CI {{S.r4.S2_D1_to_D2.sc_gaps.structure.lo}} to {{S.r4.S2_D1_to_D2.sc_gaps.structure.hi}}, but not against persistence or propensity), and the rule base is not behavioural. The abstract, highlight 4, the Discussion and the Conclusion now say this, and the Conclusion states that no rule base passes all three checks. The simulation uses the same rule. Section 7 now reads Study 2 as "compatible with a weak behavioural effect that the data cannot confirm" rather than as the true-effect scenario.

**M2. The split-half check is not Check 1.** We agree. Section 3.3, the caption of Algorithm 2 and the contribution list now call it a stability filter on the pooled direction, and say that Check 1 is applied after construction. The second highlight no longer claims the audit is built into construction. We also implemented a within-student version, SCw-TSK. In Study 2 it admits {{S.r4.S2_D1_to_D2.scw.nelig}}, {{S.r4.S2_D2_to_D1.scw.nelig}} and {{S.r4.S2_D12_to_D3.scw.nelig}} candidates and selects the same three inputs in every split. In Study 3 it selects different inputs, and neither direction beats persistence. In the simulation a noise input passes it in {{S.syn4_noise_within}}% of replications, so neither filter replaces Check 1.

**M3. Rule tables that contradict the selected directions.** Figures 3b and 4b now show SM-TSK tables, labelled as relative scores. The unconstrained consequents are in Supplementary S5. Section 5.2 lists all five SC-TSK violations as the reviewer counted them and states that the duration pair {{S.ta.z[0]}} → {{S.ta.z[1]}} involves well-supported cells. The Study 3 description no longer reports the {{S.r1.z[4]}} cell as a finding: the SM-TSK cells resting on {{S.sup3[4]}} and {{S.sup3[5]}} requests are described as extrapolated.

**M4. Contribution.**
- The contribution list now says plainly that the audit's ingredients are established practice [19,20,37]. It names what is specific to fuzzy rule bases: rule support, consequent sign violations, output monotonicity on test data, and SM-TSK with Proposition 1. It also states that Proposition 1 is a known type of condition [31,33].
- We added two comparators. A TSK rule base with three terms per input (27 rules) reaches {{S.r4.S2_D1_to_D2.tsk3.auc.e}} on D2. Monotone gradient boosting with constraints in the selected directions reaches {{S.r4.S2_D1_to_D2.mono_gbm.auc.e}}. Neither changes any verdict (Section 8, Table S7).
- We did not run FURIA, because we found no maintained implementation outside Weka; Section 8 says so.
- First-order consequents were not run. The three-term variant already tests the grid, and a first-order consequent would lose the monotonicity guarantee of Proposition 1.

**M5. Simulation.**
- The scenarios are renamed A (structure), B (propensity), C (persistence) and D (true effect).
- A mixed design (plan R4-E) adds a within-student effect β ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1} to each confound, with 3–5 or 6–10 requests per student, 100 replications per condition and 500 bootstrap resamples. Results are in Figure 5 and Table S9.
- With β = 0 the audit calls x1 behavioural in at most {{S.syn4_fp_max}}% of replications; naive transfer does so in {{S.syn4_naive_ab}}% under the structural and propensity confounds.
- Power under the structural confound is {{S.syn4['A_0.25_3-5'].all_checks}}–{{S.syn4['A_0.25_6-10'].all_checks}}% at β = 0.25, {{S.syn4['A_0.5_3-5'].all_checks}}–{{S.syn4['A_0.5_6-10'].all_checks}}% at 0.5 and 100% at 1.
- An important finding the reviewer anticipated: under the persistence confound the audit is conservative. Even at β = 1 it detects the effect in only {{S.syn4['C_1.0_3-5'].all_checks}}% and {{S.syn4['C_1.0_6-10'].all_checks}}% of replications. Sections 7 and 10 now say that Study 3's failure of Check 2 means that no behavioural effect was shown, not that none exists.
