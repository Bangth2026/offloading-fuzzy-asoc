# Major comments

**M1. Structure baseline in Study 3.** We agree that the cross-course structure model was close to degenerate. We added (a) a structure baseline restricted to variables meaningful across courses (position in session, essay length at the start of the session): AUC {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.restr.e}} (CI {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.restr.lo}} to {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.restr.hi}}), with SC-TSK gaining {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.restr_gain.e}} (CI {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.restr_gain.lo}} to {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.restr_gain.hi}}) over it; (b) within-course five-fold cross-validation grouped by student, where the full structure model is estimable: structure versus SC-TSK is {{S.wc.SW.st.e}} vs {{S.wc.SW.sc.e}} in SW, {{S.wc.AW.st.e}} vs {{S.wc.AW.sc.e}} in AW (gain {{S.wc.AW.gain.e}}, CI {{S.wc.AW.gain.lo}} to {{S.wc.AW.gain.hi}}, not reliable), {{S.wc.IRW.st.e}} vs {{S.wc.IRW.sc.e}} in IRW, and {{S.wc.ALL.st.e}} vs {{S.wc.ALL.sc.e}} pooled (gain {{S.wc.ALL.gain.e}}, CI {{S.wc.ALL.gain.lo}} to {{S.wc.ALL.gain.hi}}); (c) the variables usable in each split are now listed in Section 6.2 and Table 4. The gain over structure survives, but M3 changes its meaning (below).

**M2. Student as a structural cell.** Check 1 now includes within-student rank correlations for every selected input (Section 3.1; Sections 5.2 and 6.2), and a student-propensity baseline is reported in Tables 3 and 4. In TutorTrace D1 all three inputs keep their sign within students (ρ = {{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.ws.pre_net_code_growth.tr}}, {{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.ws.pre_chars_inserted.tr}}, {{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.ws.pre_duration_s.tr}}; all intervals exclude zero), and the SC-TSK score correlates with the label within students on D2 ({{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.ws_score.r}}, CI {{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.ws_score.lo}} to {{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.ws_score.hi}}). Propensity reaches {{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.auc_propensity.e}}, {{S.q.S2_D2_to_D1.auc_propensity.e}} and {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.auc_propensity.e}} in the three Study 2 splits and {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.auc_propensity.e}} and {{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.auc_propensity.e}} in Study 3. We did not fit a random-intercept model; the propensity baseline answers the same question with information available at prediction time.

**M3. Lagged labels and persistence.** This was the most consequential comment. The one-line Markov baseline ("the previous request was a production request") reaches {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.auc_markov.e}} and {{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.auc_markov.e}} in Study 3; SC-TSK exceeds it by {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.sc_minus_markov.e}} (CI {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.sc_minus_markov.lo}} to {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.sc_minus_markov.hi}}) and {{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.sc_minus_markov.e}}. Without the lagged-label input the re-selected rule base falls to {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.nolag.e}} and {{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.nolag.e}}. We have rewritten Study 3, the abstract, the highlights and the conclusion to say that the RECIPE4U rule base mainly describes persistence of the student's own requests. Section 6.1 now explains why lagged labels are admitted there but query length is not admitted in Study 2: the lagged label describes earlier requests whose outcome the system has already observed, whereas the length of the current query is part of the text from which the current label is assigned; it also states that deployment would need an intent classifier whose errors would propagate. In Study 2 the persistence baseline is weak ({{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.auc_markov.e}}, {{S.q.S2_D2_to_D1.auc_markov.e}}, {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.auc_markov.e}}), and on the new task SC-TSK exceeds it by {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.sc_minus_markov.e}} (CI {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.sc_minus_markov.lo}} to {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.sc_minus_markov.hi}}).

**M4. Overlap with structure.** Correlations of every selected input with every structural variable are in r2_stats.json and summarised in the text. In Study 2 the largest is |ρ| = {{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.maxov}}, so no input duplicates structure. In Study 3 the number of earlier turns is identical in rank to position in session (|ρ| = {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.maxov}}). With it removed, H2 still holds: gain {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.h2nd.e}} (CI {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.h2nd.lo}} to {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.h2nd.hi}}) and {{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.h2nd.e}} (CI {{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.h2nd.lo}} to {{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.h2nd.hi}}). In the primary split no eligible replacement remained, so the best-ranked ineligible input was used; this is stated in Section 6.2 and Appendix A.

**M5. "Pre-registered".** We agree and have removed the word from the title, abstract and highlights. Section 3.2 and Table 1 now separate what is verifiable from what is not. Using GitHub's push-event log, the Study 3 and R2 plans can be shown to have reached the public host before their analyses ran; the Study 2 plan was on GitHub before its result was pushed; E7–E12 and R1–R1c rest on local commit timestamps only (the repository was created at 07:34 UTC on 4 November). On E10: its plan (06:42 UTC) and result (06:51 UTC) both precede the earlier draft archived on Zenodo at 07:38 UTC, so the draft reported E10's result rather than E10 re-running a known analysis; the Introduction now says so. E1–E6 are listed as having decision rules in code but no separate plan, and the sentence "All analyses were specified in writing" has been removed. Section 3.2 states that Study 3's plan copied Study 2's after Study 2's results were known.

**M6. Monotone consequents.** (a) Cell support (firing degree ≥ 0.5) is now shown in Figures 3b and 4b, and Table 5 counts cells with fewer than ten requests. (b) We added SM-TSK, the same bounded least squares with linear monotonicity constraints for every pair of rules differing in one input (Section 3.3), solved with SLSQP. (c) Its AUC is a row in Tables 3 and 4. SC-TSK had {{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.viol_sc}}, {{S.q.S2_D2_to_D1.viol_sc}} and {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.viol_sc}} violations in Study 2 and {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.viol_sc}} in each Study 3 split; SM-TSK has none. SM-TSK costs 0.01 AUC or less in four of five splits ({{S.q.S2_D1_to_D2.auc_sm.e}}, {{S.q.S2_D2_to_D1.auc_sm.e}}, {{S.q.S2_D12_to_D3.auc_sm.e}}, {{S.q.S3_SW_to_AW_IRW.auc_sm.e}}) and more in the Study 3 secondary split ({{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.auc_sm.e}} vs {{S.q.S3_AW_IRW_to_SW.auc_sc.e}}).

**M7. Absence of within-group association.** The claim is rephrased throughout as "no within-group association larger than about {{S.eq_maxbound}} could be detected". We ran the equivalence test with |ρ| = 0.1 as the smallest effect of interest, and state in Section 4.3 that this value was chosen after the review: no input is equivalent to zero, because the 90% intervals reach {{S.eq_maxbound}}.

**M8. Contribution to soft computing.** We have made three changes. Two checks are now part of rule construction: the sign check selects antecedents (Section 3.3, SC-TSK) and the monotone programme constrains consequents (SM-TSK). Section 7 and Table 5 report interpretability measures from the literature in Section 2.2: number of rules, antecedents per rule, terms per input, rules with low support, sign violations and step memberships. Section 8 now opens by discussing why the audit matters more for fuzzy rule models: their output is a behavioural sentence that disguises a shortcut, and cell-wise consequents can contradict the direction that justified an input. We also say plainly that the individual checks are standard and that what is new is their combination for rule models.

**M9. User evaluation.** We could not run an instructor study in this revision. Phrases such as "the part an instructor would use" and "a clear pedagogical reading" have been removed. Section 8 now says that the tables are hypotheses about behaviour that an instructor study would have to test, and Section 9 lists the absence of user evaluation as a limitation.

**M10. Construct validity.** "Cognitive offloading" has been removed from the keywords. Section 2.3 now reviews help-seeking in intelligent tutoring: Aleven et al.'s review and their rule-based help-seeking model [25,26] and Baker et al. on gaming the system [27,28]. Studies 2 and 3 are framed as help-seeking classification, and Section 8 discusses why a dependent query or a production request need not be offloading.
